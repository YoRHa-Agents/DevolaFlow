## §1 — State of the art on AI-text detection + stylometry (2024–2026)

The published literature splits into three families: **(a)
classifier-based detectors** (GPTZero, ZeroGPT, Sapling,
Originality.ai, OpenAI's deprecated classifier); **(b) zero-shot /
contrastive detectors** (Binoculars, DetectGPT, GLTR); **(c) editorial
heuristic catalogues** (Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing", the various
humanizer / anti-detection tools). For our purpose — building a
*cheap, embedded, mechanically-applied* stylometric scorer that runs
in CI — the editorial-heuristic literature is the most useful, with
the (a)/(b) literature giving us calibration anchors and the
underlying theory.

### §1.1 — Classifier-based detectors (the GPTZero family)

The dominant production tool. Two stylometric signals form the public
core of GPTZero's algorithm:

| Signal | What it measures | Why AI is detectable |
|---|---|---|
| **Perplexity** | log-likelihood of each token under a reference LM | AI text is OPTIMIZED for low perplexity; humans pick less-probable words constantly. GPTZero reports a perplexity > 85 as "human-like". (Source: gptzero.me, 2024 marketing material; arxiv.org/pdf/2507.10475.) |
| **Burstiness** | per-sentence variance of perplexity (or sentence length) | Humans alternate short punchy sentences with long flowing ones; AI is rhythmically uniform. (Source: gptzero.me; "Can You Detect the Difference?" arxiv 2507.10475.) |

Limits of perplexity-based detectors (which is why we don't replicate
this in v10.1.0):

- They need a reference LM (e.g., GPT-2). We can't ship one in CI.
- They are LATENT-LM-dependent. New LMs (LLaDA-class diffusion
  models, 2024–2025) match human perplexity / burstiness patterns
  closely, breaking AR-trained detectors. (Source:
  arxiv.org/pdf/2507.10475 §4.)
- False-positive rates are non-zero on edited human prose.

**Implication for v10.1.0:** we use **sentence-length burstiness**
(stddev / mean of sentence length, no LM needed) as a cheap proxy
for the GPTZero burstiness concept. We do **not** attempt
perplexity.

### §1.2 — Zero-shot detectors (the Binoculars family)

Binoculars (Hans, Goldstein, Geiping et al., ICML 2024;
[arxiv.org/abs/2401.12070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12070);
github.com/ahans30/Binoculars) is the SOTA zero-shot detector. It
contrasts the perplexity of a candidate text under TWO related LMs
(e.g., Falcon-7B vs Falcon-7B-instruct) and uses the ratio. State-of-
the-art numbers: 90%+ true-positive at 0.01% false-positive on
ChatGPT-generated text, no training data needed.

The technique requires running TWO 7B-parameter LMs at inference time
to score every document. It is **categorically too expensive to run
in CI** for our use case. We treat Binoculars as the "ground truth"
that an offline calibration pass might use ONCE (PV-04 OPTIONAL) but
not as a primary scoring mechanism.

(Side note: we attempted to clone the Binoculars reference
implementation at `/home/agent/reference/Binoculars/`; the clone
timed out at 60s. Ample web-survey context covers our PV-01 needs
without the local clone.)

### §1.3 — Editorial heuristic catalogues (the Wikipedia "Signs of AI Writing" tradition)

This is the family that maps directly to a CI-embeddable scorer. The
two reference catalogues we cloned and distilled:

#### `jpeggdev/humanize-writing` (cloned: `/home/agent/reference/humanize-writing/`)

A Claude Code skill that catalogues AI tells across 8 dimensions
(structure, significance inflation, vocabulary, grammar, rhythm,
hedging, transitions, soul). Its `references/ai-tells.md` is the
single most-comprehensive Tier-1 / Tier-2 vocabulary list we found
(documented in §1.6 below).

#### `stephenturner/skill-deslop` (cloned: `/home/agent/reference/skill-deslop/`)

Companion catalogue with 4 reference docs: `phrases.md`,
`structures.md`, `tropes.md`, `examples.md`. The `structures.md`
file is the load-bearing one — it catalogues the 7 highest-signal
structural patterns (binary contrasts, negative listing, dramatic
fragmentation, self-posed rhetorical questions, anaphora abuse,
tricolon abuse, false ranges).

These two catalogues converge on the same ~12 structural / ~30
lexical tells. The convergence is high evidence that the set is
real — both authors arrived at it independently from different
samples.

### §1.4 — The 2026 "em-dash off the hook" finding

A 2026 survey by writehuman.ai (cited in §6 of the gap analysis web
search) is the most recent shift in the field. They analysed 80,141
AI writing samples + their humanized counterparts:

| Tell | AI sample density | Humanized density | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| em-dash `—` | 18.5% | 7.1% | 2.6× — "meaningful, not overwhelming" |
| `rather than` | 17,251 | 6,859 | ~2.5× |
| "ensures / ensuring" | high | low | hedging-verb cluster |
| sentence-length variance | low | higher | the "burstiness" gap |

The finding: **em-dash density is real but not the dominant tell**
in 2026 — it's been overtaken by hedging-verb clusters
("ensuring", "highlights", "supports", "reflects") and formulaic
sentence patterns ("plays a crucial role in shaping").

For DevolaFlow specifically, the empirical baseline (§3) shows that
em-dash density IS our second-strongest tell (DevolaFlow 15.4 vs
human 7.8 per 1000 words), so we keep it as a first-class
metric — but we also instrument the hedging-verb cluster to capture
the 2026 shift.

### §1.5 — The 12 most-commonly-cited stylometric features

Distilling §1.1 + §1.2 + §1.3 + §1.4 plus the web survey, the
following 12 features dominate the AI-detection literature:

| # | Feature | First-class in our scorer? | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Perplexity** under reference LM | NO | requires LM; too expensive for CI. |
| 2 | **Sentence-length burstiness** (stddev / mean) | YES | cheap proxy for GPTZero burstiness; pure stdlib. |
| 3 | **Type-Token Ratio** / **MTLD** | YES (TTR sliding-window; MTLD deferred to PV-03) | lexical diversity; cheap. |
| 4 | **Em-dash density** | YES | real signal in our docs; 2× over human baseline. |
| 5 | **Bullet-list density** (line-fraction with bullet markers) | YES | the user's #1 structural complaint surfaced in our worst docs (CHANGELOG: 0.628). |
| 6 | **Header-to-prose ratio** | YES | directly observable; AI-generated docs over-section. |
| 7 | **Bold inline density** (`**...**` per 1k words) | YES | DevolaFlow's CHANGELOG runs 34/1k vs human 12/1k. |
| 8 | **Tier-1 AI vocabulary** ("delve", "tapestry", etc.) | YES | classic detection signal; 32 tokens (humanize-writing). |
| 9 | **Tier-2 AI vocabulary** ("robust", "comprehensive", etc.) | YES | 38 tokens; weighted lower than Tier-1. |
| 10 | **Signposting / hedging phrases** ("It's worth noting", etc.) | YES | 45 multi-word patterns. |
| 11 | **Negative parallelism** ("not X but Y", "X isn't Y, it's Z") | YES | the 2026 single-most-cited tell; 6 regex patterns. |
| 12 | **Copula-avoidance** ("serves as", "stands as", "represents") | YES (advisory weight only) | clusters are AI; isolated occurrences are normal. |

### §1.7 — Signposting + parallelism patterns

45 signposting / hedging multi-word phrases distilled from the same
two reference catalogues. Categories:

- **"It's worth..." family**: it's worth noting, it's important to
  note, it's crucial to understand, it bears mentioning
- **"In today's..." family**: in today's fast-paced world, in today's
  digital landscape, in today's competitive market
- **"At its core..." family**: at its core, at the end of the day,
  when all is said and done
- **Hedging starters**: while there are certainly, to be sure, that
  said, this is not without
- **AI closers**: the bottom line is, only time will tell, the
  future is bright, exciting times lie ahead
- **Filler phrases**: in order to, due to the fact that, at this
  point in time, in the event that, has the ability to
- **Sycophantic / chatbot artifacts**: great question, certainly!,
  absolutely!, i hope this helps, let me know if you'd like
- **Pedagogical hand-holding**: let's break this down, let's unpack,
  let's explore, let's dive in, let's delve, think of it as, think
  of it like, imagine a world where
- **Generic transitions**: in conclusion, to sum up, in summary,
  moreover, furthermore, additionally, also,, notably, importantly,
  interestingly, crucially
- **"Despite challenges" formula**: despite challenges, despite
  these challenges
- **Throat-clearing openers**: here's the thing, here's what i find,
  here's the kicker, here's the deal, here's why, here's where it
  gets interesting

The 6 negative-parallelism patterns (the single highest-signal 2026
tell):

```
not just X but (also) Y
isn't (about) X, it's/but Y
not only X but also Y
it's not (about|just) X, it's Y
the (answer|question|problem) isn't X, it's Y
not X. not Y. (just|it's) Z              # countdown reveal
```

The 7 copula-avoidance patterns (clustering-aware; weight low
because individual occurrences are normal):

```
serves? as
stands? as
represents? a/the/an
marks? a/the/an
boasts? a/the/an/<digit>
features? a/the/an/<digit>
offers? a/the/an
```

### §2.3 — Key takeaways (for PV-02 design)

From `humanize-writing/SKILL.md` (464 lines of editorial process):

1. **The fix is rarely a synonym substitution.** Most lines that
   trigger an AI tell need restructuring, not word replacement. This
   is the load-bearing constraint on transformer scope (§5 below): we
   can mechanically delete clear filler ("it's worth noting that
   X" → "X") but we cannot mechanically rewrite "X serves as Y" to
   "X is Y" without ruining sentences where "serves as" is correct
   ("the museum serves as both archive and gallery").
2. **8-pass editorial sequence:** Structure → Inflation → Vocabulary
   → Grammar → Rhythm → Hedging → Transitions → Soul. v10.1.0 should
   adopt 3 of 8 (Vocabulary delete-list + Hedging delete-list +
   Em-dash dedupe) as MECHANICAL transforms. The other 5 require
   human judgment and must NOT be auto-applied.
3. **"Pattern stacking" is one tell, not three.** When boldface +
   scare-quotes + em-dash all hit the same coined term, that's ONE
   tell. The PV-01 probe doesn't currently dedupe this; PV-03 should.
4. **Read-it-out-loud test:** "if no human would actually say it in
   conversation, it's a tell." Cannot be mechanised. **Off-limits
   for v10.1.0.**

From `skill-deslop/references/structures.md` (185 lines of structural
patterns):

1. **The single most-cited 2026 AI tell:** binary contrasts /
   negative parallelism. v10.1.0 instruments this; the PV-01
   baseline shows DevolaFlow scores 0.0 here (we already don't do
   this — confirms the user complaint is structural-density not
   structural-trope).
2. **Dramatic fragmentation** ("This unlocks something. Period.")
   and **anaphora abuse** ("They assume X. They assume Y.") — both
   ZERO in DevolaFlow docs. We're clean on these.
3. **Tricolon abuse** — present in some DevolaFlow docs but cannot
   be mechanically detected without semantic analysis (a
   one-third-padding tricolon needs to know what counts as
   "padding"). Off-limits.

From `chezmoi/README.md` (only 17 lines but useful as a "minimal"
target): a 17-line README scoring 75 on naturalness IS achievable
when prose density is low and bullets are sparse. The DevolaFlow
README is 535 lines; it cannot reach 75 by simple subtraction. **The
goal for v10.1.0 is to lift the worst 5–7 docs by ≥ 10 points each,
not to reach human-clean parity uniformly.**

## §3 — Empirical characterisation of DevolaFlow's prose

### §3.1 — Probe methodology

`.local/research/v10.1.0_aivoice_probe.py` (635 lines, stdlib-only)
computes 16 features per document. Preprocessing:

1. Strip HTML `<script>` / `<style>` blocks
2. Strip fenced code blocks ` ``` ... ``` `
3. Strip inline code spans `` `...` ``
4. Strip URLs
5. Strip HTML tags
6. **For lexical features only**: also strip markdown blockquote
   lines `> ...` and table rows `| ... |` so editorial docs that
   QUOTE Tier-1 vocabulary as examples don't get falsely penalised
   for cataloguing them.

Tokenisation: ASCII word regex `[A-Za-z][A-Za-z'-]+` (lowercased).
Sentence segmentation: `(?<=[.!?。！？])\s+(?=[A-Z\u4e00-\u9fa5])`.
CJK characters counted in their own column and added 1-to-1 to the
word denominator for densities (a defensible coarse mapping; PV-03
can swap in `jieba` if ZH gating tightens).

Per-doc features computed:

```
chars  words  sentences  paragraphs  cjk_chars
avg_sent_len  sent_len_stddev  burstiness (= stddev/mean)
ttr (sliding 200-token window)
header_ratio (header lines / non-blank lines)
bullet_ratio (bullet lines / non-blank lines)
bold_per_1k  emdash_per_1k
tier1_per_1k  tier2_per_1k
signposts_per_1k  neg_parallel_per_1k  copula_dodge_per_1k
naturalness (composite 0-100)
```

Total runtime on the live corpus: ~700ms (well within the cheap-CI
budget of <2s per release-preflight pass).

### §3.5 — Five-point characterisation (the user's "AI 味" deconstructed)

The user's complaint maps to a **structural** problem in a
**concentrated** subset of docs, NOT a lexical problem in all docs.
Specifically:

1. **The em-dash is doing the heaviest lifting.** Across the worst
   7 docs, em-dash density runs 25–38 per 1000 words vs the human
   baseline of 7.8. PV-02 dedupe / collapse rules can take this to
   12–15 mechanically (the natural human cap is ~15 per 1000 in
   formal technical writing; below 8 is the "very edited" range).
2. **Bold inline overuse is a CHANGELOG-author habit.** The
   CHANGELOG runs 34 per 1k; the README runs 35 per 1k; some EN
   guides run 60+ per 1k. Human-clean baseline is 37 — close to
   the CHANGELOG, BUT the human baseline is inflated by the caveman
   README which is deliberately formatted-heavy. The cleaner human
   target (ruff 12.5, deslop-structures 25, ironclaw 73 — wide
   variance) is "10–25 per 1k". PV-02 should cap at 25 / 1k for
   non-CHANGELOG docs and 18 / 1k for the CHANGELOG.
3. **Bullet ratio is doc-class-specific.** The CHANGELOG hits 0.628
   because it's effectively a release-notes registry of 50+ bullet
   items per release. The EN guides hit 0.37–0.39 because they're
   step-by-step. Human dev-tool READMEs run 0.10–0.30. The fix is
   not "remove bullets" — it's "collapse 1-bullet and 2-bullet lists
   into prose; merge consecutive 1-line bullets that share a parent
   into a single sentence with semicolons".
4. **Lexical AI vocab is essentially absent.** Tier 1 hits across
   the entire DevolaFlow corpus = ~9 (out of 80,368 words). The
   user's "AI 味" complaint is NOT about this. We can keep the
   Tier-1 detector for guard-rail purposes but PV-02 should NOT
   prioritise vocabulary-list humanizing.
5. **Negative parallelism + copula avoidance + signposting are also
   essentially absent.** All three score 0–0.1 per 1k. We're
   already clean on these. Same conclusion as #4: keep the
   detector, deprioritise the transform.

The PV-02 design must spend its scope budget on em-dash + bold +
bullet density transforms, NOT on vocabulary substitution.

## §4 — Proposed evaluation metric (the "naturalness score")

### §4.1 — Design constraints

| Constraint | Source | Implementation rule |
|---|---|---|
| Cheap to compute | C-3 user mandate ("performance non-pollution") | stdlib only; <2s on the whole corpus; no LM inference. |
| Deterministic | W-9 SI-10 gate 1 (test-stability) | pure function over text; no random seeds; sorted iteration. |
| Cross-language | EN + ZH parity (ST-3) | works on Mandarin via CJK char count + 。！？ sentence split. |
| Editorial-corpus aware | §1.3 finding (catalogues falsely score low) | strip blockquotes + table rows from lex pass; KEEP for structural pass. |
| Calibratable | C-1 user mandate ("如何去评价 AI 味") | weights live in YAML (PV-02 design); thresholds live in `tests/` config. |
| Per-doc scoring | §3.5 finding (heavy-tailed distribution) | one score per file; aggregate is informational. |

### §4.2 — Composite formula

The score is a weighted sum of 10 sub-scores, each in `[0, 1]`
where 0 = "no AI tell on this dimension" and 1 = "saturated AI
tell". Naturalness = `100 × (1 - Σ wᵢ · sᵢ)`.

```
sub-score                       weight  threshold (range 0→1)
-------------------------------  ------  -------------------------
burstiness_ai     (target 0.65)   0.10  (0.65 - obs) / 0.65
ttr_ai            (target 0.45)   0.05  (0.45 - obs) / 0.30
bold_ai           (cap 8/1k)      0.15  (obs - 8)    / 25.0
emdash_ai         (cap 5/1k)      0.15  (obs - 5)    / 25.0
tier1_ai          (cap 0/1k)      0.05  obs / 5.0
tier2_ai          (cap 0/1k)      0.05  obs / 15.0
signposts_ai      (cap 0/1k)      0.10  obs / 10.0
neg_parallel_ai   (cap 0/1k)      0.05  obs / 3.0
bullet_ai         (cap 0.25)      0.15  (obs - 0.25) / 0.25
header_ai         (cap 0.12)      0.15  (obs - 0.12) / 0.15
                                  ----
                                  1.00
```

The `cap` row is the threshold below which the sub-score is 0
(no penalty); above the cap each unit increment scales the
sub-score linearly until it saturates at 1.

Weights rationale:

- `bullet`, `header`, `bold`, `emdash` get **0.15 each (60%
  total)** because §3.5 identifies them as the dominant
  DevolaFlow tell.
- `signposts` gets **0.10** as the next-tier tell.
- `burstiness` gets **0.10** as the GPTZero proxy.
- `tier1`, `tier2`, `neg_parallel`, `ttr` get **0.05 each** —
  detectors kept for guard-rail purposes; not the primary
  v10.1.0 fix surface.

| `AGG:devolaflow` | 69.96 | BASELINE. v10.1.0 release demands this rises by ≥ 3 points (target 73+). |

Calibration knobs PV-02 may turn:

- **Per-doc-class weights**: the CHANGELOG has structural
  characteristics that don't apply to a quickstart guide. PV-02
  may emit `{"changelog": {bold_cap: 18}, "default": {bold_cap: 25}}`.
- **Soft floor vs hard floor**: a soft floor at 65 (CI warning,

- **Pinned baselines**: golden YAML at
  `benchmarks/devolaflow_context/baselines/aivoice_v10.1.0.yaml`

### §4.4 — Calibration loop (PV-04 OPTIONAL)

If we want to validate the weights empirically (rather than
intuitively), PV-04 can run an offline LLM-judge calibration:

1. Sample 30 paragraphs from across the DevolaFlow corpus +
   30 from human-clean.
2. Ask Claude / GPT-4 (offline; not in CI): "rate this paragraph
   1–10 for AI-flavor, where 10 = clearly AI."
3. Fit naturalness vs LLM-judged score; adjust weights to
   maximise Spearman correlation.

This is OPTIONAL and offline. NEVER goes in CI per C-3.

## §5 — Catalogue of mechanical transformation rules

Three tiers: **safe** (mechanical, low semantic risk),
**risky** (mechanical-able but high false-positive risk),
**off-limits** (require human judgment; v10.1.0 must NOT auto-apply).

### §5.1 — Safe transforms (PV-03 first batch)

| # | Transform | Mechanical rule | Risk | Test fixture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-S1 | **Em-dash dedupe** | Replace ` -- ` with ` — ` (typographic normalisation, not a tell-fix); then COLLAPSE consecutive em-dash asides — if a paragraph has 3+ em-dashes, downgrade the 2nd through Nth to commas. | LOW | `t_em_dash_collapse_fixture.md` |
| T-S2 | **Bullet collapse for 1-item / 2-item lists** | Detect `^- ` lines forming a list of length ≤ 2 and the parent paragraph; collapse them into prose using "X, Y" or "X (e.g., Y)". Skip if any bullet is longer than 80 chars (likely a real list, not collapsable). | LOW | `t_bullet_collapse_fixture.md` |
| T-S3 | **Signpost-strip** (PV-03 starter set) | Delete the 12 highest-confidence signposts when they open a sentence: "It's worth noting that ", "It's important to note that ", "In today's fast-paced world, ", "At its core, ", "The bottom line is, ", "In conclusion, ", "To sum up, ", "Moreover, ", "Furthermore, ", "Additionally, ", "Notably, ", "Importantly, ". Cut just the prefix; preserve the rest of the sentence. | LOW | `t_signpost_strip_fixture.md` |
| T-S4 | **Bold-density cap** | If `bold_per_1k > cap` for a doc, strip bold from inline-list-header items first (`- **Foo:** bar`) until under cap. NEVER touch bold inside `## Headers`. | LOW-MED | `t_bold_cap_fixture.md` |
| T-S5 | **Sycophantic-opener delete** | Drop "Great question!", "Certainly!", "Absolutely!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like" wholesale — these never appear in DevolaFlow's actual docs but should be a guard-rail in case future agents inherit this skill. | LOW | `t_sycophant_delete_fixture.md` |

All 5 safe transforms must satisfy the PV-03 implementation
contract: **byte-stable on a fixture with embedded code blocks**.

### §5.2 — Risky transforms (PV-03 design only; ship in v10.1.0+)

| # | Transform | Mechanical rule | Risk | Why risky |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-R1 | Tier-1 vocab substitution | "delve" → "examine", "leverage" → "use", "harness" → "use", etc. | MED | Some uses are correct ("the museum's collection delves into local history" is NOT a tell). Need POS tagging or context window to avoid false positives. |
| T-R2 | Copula-dodge unwinding | "X serves as Y" → "X is Y" when within a cluster of ≥ 3 dodges. | MED | "serves as" is correct in formal contexts ("the model serves as a baseline"). Cluster detection is fragile. |
| T-R3 | Negative parallelism rewrite | "It's not just X, it's Y" → "It's Y" (drop the negation). | HIGH | Sometimes the negation IS the rhetorical point. Even one false-positive is a high cost. |
| T-R4 | "From X to Y" false-range collapse | "From the singularity of the Big Bang to the grand cosmic web" → list X and Y separately. | HIGH | Cannot mechanically distinguish a real spectrum from a false one without semantic understanding. |

### §5.3 — Off-limits transforms (NEVER auto-apply)

| # | Transform | Why off-limits | What we do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-OL1 | Header restructuring | Headers are part of the doc's information architecture. Restructuring requires understanding what the doc is about. | The naturalness scorer FLAGS over-headered docs; humans fix. |
| T-OL2 | Section reordering | Same as T-OL1. | Same. |
| T-OL3 | Code-block content rewriting | Code is contracts. NEVER edit. | Probe / scorer SKIP code blocks via `_strip_code_and_html()`; transformer must obey the same boundary. |
| T-OL4 | API signature paraphrasing | Signatures are contracts. | Same as T-OL3. |
| T-OL5 | CLI invocation rewriting | Same as T-OL3. | Same. |
| T-OL6 | Version-string normalisation | CP-3 already handles this. | n/a — this is `bump_version.py`'s job. |
| T-OL7 | Tone shift (formal → casual) | The user's "human voice" is NOT "casual voice". Mistaking the two would CREATE a new AI-flavor signal (forced colloquialisms read as worse than over-formal). | Keep the existing register; reduce density / signposting only. |
| T-OL8 | Adding "I" / first-person | First-person is fine in some docs (cycle retrospectives) and wrong in others (quickstart). Cannot mechanically decide. | Off-limits. |
| T-OL9 | Adding "humour" or "personality" | The humanize-writing SKILL §"Soul" says explicitly "don't add humour that doesn't serve the point". Cannot mechanise. | Off-limits. |

### §5.4 — Transformation policy summary for PV-02

PV-02 design must commit to:

1. **Ship 3–5 of the SAFE transforms in v10.1.0 PV-03.**
   Recommended starter set: T-S1 (em-dash) + T-S2 (bullet
   collapse) + T-S3 (signpost-strip) + T-S5 (sycophantic
   guard-rail). T-S4 (bold cap) is a strong candidate but
   needs more design care because it interacts with table
   rendering.
2. **DO NOT ship any RISKY transforms in v10.1.0.** Defer
   T-R1..T-R4 to v10.2.0+ when we have the offline LLM-judge
   calibration data to ground them.
3. **Codify the OFF-LIMITS list as a `writing_style/POLICY.md`
   doc.** It's the contract that prevents future cycles from
   accidentally enabling T-OL1..T-OL9.

## §6 — Binary-choice items L0 must resolve before PV-02 design

These are decisions that materially shape PV-02's scope. They cannot
be resolved by an L1 research agent — they are L0 / human-user
calls.

### Q-A — Module placement: `src/devolaflow/writing_style/` vs `src/devolaflow/local/style/`

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **(A) `src/devolaflow/writing_style/`** as a new top-level package | Maximally isolated from hot path; easy to lint that no hot-path module imports from it; intuitive name (matches the SKILL chooser's expectations); future-extensible (we can add `writing_style/scorer.py`, `writing_style/transforms/`, `writing_style/POLICY.md`). | A new top-level adds another package to the audit surface; v10.0.0 retro §3.5 telegraphed "Parallel Wave Dispatch Invariant" as a v10.2.0+ candidate — we need package real-estate. |
| **(B) `src/devolaflow/local/style/`** under the existing `local/` namespace (compiler / etc) | Reuses existing namespace; suggests "local-only / not-runtime-hot"; aligns with `local/compiler` pattern. | The `local/` namespace has historically been compiler-adjacent (rule compiler at `local/compiler.py`); adding writing-style transforms there muddies its semantic. |

**Recommendation:** **(A)**. Cleaner separation; matches user
mandate C-3 ("performance non-pollution") most clearly; passes
the static lint test (R-4).

### Q-B — Transform timing: at-generation-time vs at-commit-time

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **(A) At generation** — the transformer runs as a post-process inside `scripts/generate_human_docs.py` BEFORE the file is written to disk. | Single-source: every regen produces a transformed doc. CI gate (`tests/test_check_drift_cmd`) auto-validates byte-stability of the transformed output. Authors who write the SKILL never see the transformer's effect on EN/ZH guides; they edit SKILL freely. | The transformer affects only auto-generated guides — the README and CHANGELOG are hand-edited and don't go through `generate_human_docs.py`. We'd need a SECOND surface for them. |
| **(B) At commit** — pre-commit hook reads target docs, transforms them, writes back. | Covers EVERY hand-edited doc (README, CHANGELOG, EN/ZH if hand-touched). No special-casing. | Authors see their CHANGELOG entry rewritten on commit — surprising; potentially destructive. Requires `pre-commit` install / setup. |
| **(C) BOTH** — the at-generation pass for `generate_human_docs.py` outputs PLUS a separate `make humanize-docs` target the author can run manually for README + CHANGELOG (no auto-pre-commit). | Best of both: auto-protection on the auto-gen surface; opt-in for the hand-edited surface. CI gate (the naturalness scorer) is the SAME for both. | Two surfaces to maintain. |

**Recommendation:** **(C)**. The CI naturalness gate is the
release-side enforcer; the at-gen + manual-make surfaces are the
author-side conveniences.

### Q-C — Tone profile count: 1 vs 3

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **(A) ONE profile: "natural"** | Simpler. Fewer YAML knobs. Matches the user's "human voice" framing literally. | A README and a CHANGELOG and a quickstart all have different voice expectations; a single profile under-fits. |
| **(B) THREE profiles**: `technical_concise` (CHANGELOG + research artefacts), `documentation_natural` (EN/ZH guides + README), `marketing_warm` (demo HTML "What's New" sections + landing page). | Lets per-doc-class thresholds (Q-D below) attach naturally. | More YAML; more design surface; risk of profile-leak (a doc gets the wrong profile). |

**Recommendation:** **(B)**. The CHANGELOG and the quickstart
empirically need different bullet/bold caps (the CHANGELOG runs
0.628 bullet; the quickstart 0.379 — both should drop, but to
different floors).

### Q-D — CHANGELOG-entry policy: transformed vs untouched (ref-doc treatment)

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **(A) CHANGELOG entries go through the transformer** | Consistency: every release has a "natural" entry. Auto-fixes the worst-naturalness doc in the corpus. | Authors are used to writing CHANGELOG entries in a specific style (bullet-heavy, tag/SHA mentions, file-list tables). Transformer rewriting these breaks reading habits + Keep-a-Changelog idiom. |
| **(B) CHANGELOG is treated as a reference document** — transformer SKIPS it; the gate scorer SKIPS it; humans write directly. | Preserves the existing convention. Zero risk of breaking release notes. | The user's complaint EXPLICITLY targets the CHANGELOG (it's the worst naturalness doc); excluding it ducks the user's #1 surface. |
| **(C) Mixed: CHANGELOG goes through the transformer for LIGHT transforms (T-S1 em-dash + T-S5 sycophantic-strip) but SKIPS T-S2 (bullet collapse) and T-S3 (signpost) and T-S4 (bold cap).** | Captures the high-signal em-dash dedupe (the largest delta) without breaking the bullet-table register. | Requires per-doc-class transform whitelisting (interacts with Q-C). |

**Recommendation:** **(C)**. Enable T-S1 + T-S5 on the CHANGELOG;
disable T-S2 + T-S3 + T-S4. The em-dash dedupe alone should lift
the CHANGELOG by ~5 points (52.70 → ~58); add bold-cap-soft (cap
at 25/1k instead of 18/1k) and we get to ~62.

### Q-E — Scorer as a hard gate vs advisory-only in v10.1.0

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **(A) HARD gate**: any human-facing doc with naturalness < hard_floor (e.g., 50) BLOCKS commit. | Forces authors to address regressions immediately. Mirrors the W-3 / SI-3 composite-score gate culture. | High risk of authors disabling the gate if it false-positives early. The first release with the gate is the highest-risk one. |
| **(B) ADVISORY-only** in v10.1.0; HARD gate ships in v10.2.0 after we have one cycle of empirical data. | Lower friction. Lets us tune thresholds before they become release-blocking. | Authors may ignore the warnings — the v10.1.0 cycle would ship without measurable improvement. |
| **(C) MIXED**: HARD gate at the very low floor (≤ 45) + ADVISORY warning at the soft floor (≤ 65). | Catches actual regressions (a doc dropping below 45 is unambiguously bad) while allowing graceful upgrade for existing 50–65 docs. | Two thresholds to defend in the design doc. |

**Recommendation:** **(C)**. Hard at 45 (catches regressions);
advisory at 65 (matches human-clean baseline minus 5 points
headroom).
