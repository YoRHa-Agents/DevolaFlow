# v10.1.0 PV-05 — Writing-Style Transform Measurement Report

## Verdict: SHIP

The three ship conditions defined in the PV-05 spec all pass. The
aggregate naturalness of DevolaFlow's 21 human-facing documents
moved from 69.958 to 77.664, a gain of 7.706 points, and every
doc the PV-01 probe flagged as worst-7 gained at least 10 points
after the transforms ran. No document with a pre-transform score
at or above the advisory floor of 65 regressed below it.

## Methodology

The pre-transform baseline was collected by PV-02's harness and
committed at `benchmarks/writing_style/baselines/v10.1.0_pre.json`.
It matches the PV-01 probe aggregate of 69.958 byte-for-byte, so the
scoring pipeline itself has not drifted.

The transforms ran through `make humanize-docs` over README.md,
CHANGELOG.md, and the 16 EN / ZH guides. The CHANGELOG used the
`technical_concise` profile (T-S1 em-dash + T-S5 clichés only, per
Q-D), everything else used `documentation_natural` (all five
transforms enabled). The post-transform baseline was measured by the
same runner into `benchmarks/writing_style/baselines/v10.1.0_post.json`.

## Attribution — which transforms moved which docs

The per-transform byte deltas from `make humanize-docs` show the
relative contributions:

The em-dash transform carried the CHANGELOG. Its document-level
density cap at 8 em-dashes per 1000 words (the chezmoi / ruff /
caveman human-clean baseline is 7.8) demoted roughly 1,400
em-dashes across the CHANGELOG alone. That single transform
delivered the +13.00 composite lift almost entirely on its own,
taking CHANGELOG from a worst-in-corpus 52.70 to a clean 65.70.

The headers transform was the decisive move for
df:zh/workflow-types. Because that doc is bold-saturated (bold
density near 50 per 1000 words), the headers transform's
profile-aware plain-text demotion path activates. Demoting short
`###` sections to plain text reduces the header-to-prose ratio
without pushing the already-saturated bold count any further.

The bullets transform carried most of the lift on the EN and ZH
integration guides and on en/faq. Those docs had bullet ratios
above 0.37. Collapsing their 1- and 2-item lists into prose
brought the ratio under the 0.25 cap where the scorer stops
penalising.

Signposts and clichés barely moved bytes. That confirms the PV-01
research §3.5 finding: DevolaFlow's authored prose is already
clean on lexical AI-vocab tells. Those two transforms earn their
place as guard-rails against future drift, not as active fixes.

## What the no-change docs tell us

Several documents show near-zero movement:

* `df:demo/index.html` — HTML stripping already sends most of its
  prose past the saturation cap. Nothing for transforms to grip onto.
* `df:research/v10.0.0_cycle_plan` and `df:research/v10.0.0_retrospective`
  — these live under `.local/research/` and are not targeted by
  `make humanize-docs`. They appear in the corpus because the
  benchmark runner still scores them; their 0.00 deltas simply
  reflect that the transform pipeline never touches them.
* `df:en/customization-guide` and `df:zh/customization-guide` — both
  sit around 72, comfortably above the advisory floor. The
  transforms had no structural density to compress.

All these zero-deltas are expected; none affect the gate.

## Design refinements landed during PV-05

Two transform adjustments were needed to clear the gate:

The em-dash transform gained a third stage beyond PV-03's
typographic normalisation and per-paragraph cap. Neither of those
moved CHANGELOG because release-notes entries are one em-dash
each, so the paragraph cap never fires. The new document-level
density pass walks em-dashes in reading order and keeps only the
first N under an 8-per-1000-word budget. The rest become ", ". This
aligns the post-transform density with the human-clean corpus
mean.

The headers transform shifted from per-region to document scope.
The region-aware approach counted body word counts only within a
single prose region, which is short when inline-code or links
fragment the document. The whole-`## Built-in Workflow Types ##`
section in the README legitimately has 229 body words but its
prose region was only 152 chars, making the transform
mis-classify it as an orphan. Document-scope counting respects
markdown semantics without giving up region-safety for header
lines themselves (those are pure markdown syntax with no
protected spans inside them). The transform also limits itself to
H3 and deeper — H2 headers are coarse section structure authors
deliberately set up, never demoted.
